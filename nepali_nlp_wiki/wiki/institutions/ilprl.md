# Information and Language Processing Research Lab (ILPRL)

## Overview
The **Information and Language Processing Research Lab (ILPRL)**, established under the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at Kathmandu University (KU), is one of Nepal's foremost academic bodies conducting research in computational linguistics, machine translation, speech synthesis, and Devanagari OCR.

## Primary Research Initiatives
- **DOBHASE Translation Platform**: Landmark English-to-Nepali rule-based and statistical machine translation systems.
- **Speech Synthesis (TTS) & ASR**: Grapheme-to-phoneme mapping, prosodic modeling, and deep learning for spoken Nepali.
- **Nepali WordNet & Semantics**: Collaborative curation of Nepali synsets within the IndoWordNet framework.
- **Optical Character Recognition**: Tesseract adaptations and Shirorekha-removal algorithms for Devanagari prints.

## Key Leadership & Researchers
- **Prof. Dr. Bal Krishna Bal**: Lab Director, Professor at KU DoCSE, and pioneer of Nepali computational linguistics. Author of seminal works on Nepali finite-state morphology, the Nelralec corpus, and DOBHASE MT architecture.
- **Faculty & Alumni Collaborators**: Sunil Regmi, Rupak Raj Ghimire, Prakash Poudyal, Praveen Acharya, Bipesh Subedi, Surendrabikram Thapa, Pravesh Koirala, Rupesh Shrestha, Roop Shree Ratna Bajracharya, and Santosh Regmi.

## Historical Research Trajectory (2004–2026)
1. **Foundations (2004–2008)**:
   - Morphological analysis, stemmers, and spellcheckers (Bal & Shrestha, 2004, 2007).
   - Structure of Nepali grammar and dictionary-based spellcheckers (Bal, 2004).
   - MPP 43-tag Part-of-Speech tagset design and Nelralec corpus development (Prasain, Khatiwada, Bal, & Shrestha, 2008).
2. **Semantics, Opinion Mining & Early OCR (2009–2016)**:
   - Argumentation and opinion mining in Nepali editorials (Bal & Saint-Dizier, 2009, 2010; Bal, 2009).
   - Early OCR initiatives and Shirorekha-preprocessing for printed Devanagari (Pant & Bal, 2016).
   - Subjectivity analysis and fact-vs-opinion classification (Regmi & Bal, 2015, 2017).
3. **Machine Learning, Speech & Low-Resource Translation (2018–2021)**:
   - Comparative statistical vs neural machine translation (Acharya & Bal, 2018).
   - Concatenative and parametric Nepali speech synthesis (TTS) (Bajracharya et al., 2018).
   - Named entity recognition (EverestNER / Maharjan et al., 2019) and aspect-based sentiment analysis (Tamrakar et al., 2020; Singh et al., 2020).
   - Low-resource Tamang-Nepali machine translation (Chaudhary, Bal, Baidar, 2020).
   - End-to-end Nepali ASR systems using ESPnet (Regmi & Bal, 2021).
4. **Transformers & Multimodal Processing (2022–2024)**:
   - Monolingual transformer evaluation (NetBERT, NepBERTa) for Nepali NLU (Maskey et al., 2022).
   - Vision-language grounding: Nepali image captioning and video captioning (Subedi & Bal, 2022; Subedi et al., 2023).
   - Pronunciation-aware syllable tokenizers and active learning for ASR (Ghimire et al., 2023).
   - Historical script recognition for Ranjana / Nepal Script using CRNN-CTC (Nakarmi et al., 2024).
   - LLMs for low-resource Nepali POS and NER (Subedi, Regmi, Bal, Acharya, 2024).
5. **Modern Benchmarks, Legal NLP & Speech Architectures (2025–2026)**:
   - NLUE: 12-task standardized natural language understanding benchmark (Nyachhyon, Sharma, Thapa, Bal, 2025).
   - NepConformer: Conformer-based speech recognition and Rule-Based Character Constituency Loss (Poudel et al., 2025; Ghimire et al., 2025).
   - Trilingual English-Nepali-Tamang parallel corpora and benchmarks (NepTam / Acharya et al., 2026).
   - Legal domain NLP: NepLEGiT pretrained legal transformers and NepKANUN legal retrieval assistant (Raut et al., 2026; Thapa et al., 2026).
   - Low-resource Devanagari information retrieval benchmark (Acharya & Bal, ACM SIGIR 2026).
