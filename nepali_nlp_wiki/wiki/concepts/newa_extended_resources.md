# Nepal Bhasa (Newar) Manuscripts, Epigraphy & Extended Literary Heritage

## Overview
This concept node compiles the extended historical and philological manuscript holdings of Nepal Bhasa, establishing the baseline corpora for historical linguistics, paleography, and digital epigraphy.

---

## 1. Landmark Cambridge University Digital Library (CUDL) Manuscripts

1. **Mānavanyāyaśāstraṭīkā (1407 AD / NS 527 - MS Add.2137)**:
   - Written in Patan under King Jayasthiti Malla by scribe Māṇikya.
   - Oldest secular legal commentary in Classical Newari; indispensable for medieval commercial, civic, and penal terminology.
2. **Amarakośavivṛti (1386 AD / NS 506 - MS Add.1698)**:
   - Written in Bhaktapur; bilingual Newari gloss on the Sanskrit *Amarakośa*, establishing medieval lexical definitions.
3. **Classical Newari Hitopadeśa (MS Add.1591)**:
   - Interlinear vernacular prose translation of Sanskrit fables, demonstrating early SOV syntactic alignments.
4. **Kalāpustaka (17th Century - MS Add.864)**:
   - Polychrome accordion manuscript (*thyāsaphū*) with narrative descriptions in Nepālākṣarā script.
5. **Pañcarakṣā (1205 / 1459 AD - MS Add.1644)**:
   - Palm-leaf Buddhist dharani manuscript containing 15th-century historical Newari colophons.

---

## 2. Inscriptions & Royal Chronicles (*Vaṃśāvalī*)

- **Gopālarājavaṃśāvalī (गोपालराजवंशावली, c. 1387–1390 AD)**:
   - Vellum manuscript chronicle kept in the National Archives of Nepal (Rashtriya Abhilekhalaya).
   - Foundational source for medieval Nepal Valley history, earthquakes, royal coronations, and architectural foundations written in mixed Sanskrit and Classical Newari.
- **Malla Stone Inscriptions (*Śilāpatra*)**:
   - Thousands of stone epigraphs across Kathmandu, Bhaktapur, and Patan Durbar Squares documenting land endowments (*guthi*), temple dedications, and royal decrees in Prachalit and Ranjana scripts.

## 3. Linguistic & Lexical Authorities

- **A Dictionary of Classical Newari (2000)**:
   - Compiled by Kashinath Tamot, Ian Alsop, and Kamal P. Malla from 40,000+ manuscript citations spanning the 12th to 19th centuries.
- **Comparative Lexical Glossaries**:
   - S. Manandhar (1986): *Newari-English Dictionary*.
   - K.P. Shrestha (2000): *Newari-Nepali-English Dictionary*.
