# Text Complexity & Readability

> [!IMPORTANT] Gist
> Measuring text difficulty involves more than simple syllable counts. Modern computational models (like the L.A.S.T. Index) integrate lexical density, syntactic complexity, and propositional richness to estimate reading comprehension challenge (Liontou, 2015).

## 1. Traditional vs. Computational Readability
- **Traditional Formulas**: Flesch-Kincaid or Gunning Fog (based on sentence length and syllables per word).
- **Computational Models**: Use in-depth linguistic features like:
    - **Lexical Density**: The ratio of content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives) to total tokens.
    - **Syntactic Structure Similarity**: How similar the sentence structures are across a document (higher similarity often means easier reading).
    - **Academic Vocabulary**: The percentage of words from specialized lists (e.g., the Academic Word List).

## 2. Lexicogrammatical Complexity
- **Propositional Idea Density (PID)**: Measuring the number of ideas or "propositions" relative to the word count (CPIDR). High PID indicates a text that is information-heavy.
- **Grammatical Intricacy**: The number of clauses per sentence.
- **Word Frequency**: The use of "Word Families" and the frequency of words in general usage corpora. Rare words increase difficulty.
- **Idioms & Phrasal Verbs**: These "non-compositional" expressions (where the meaning isn't obvious from the individual words) are major hurdles for L2 learners.

## 3. Cohesion & Coherence
Readability is also affected by how well the text "sticks together":
- **Halliday & Hasan's Model**: Focusing on **Referential Cohesion** (pronouns), **Lexical Cohesion** (synonyms), and **Conjunctions**.
- **Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA)**: Used to measure the semantic overlap between sentences.

## 4. Reader Variables
Text difficulty is not just a property of the text; it is an interaction with the reader:
- **Content Schemata**: The reader's background knowledge about the topic.
- **Formal Schemata**: The reader's knowledge of the text structure (e.g., knowing how a lab report is organized).
- **Topic Preference**: Highly motivated readers can often tackle texts above their "official" level.
