# Lexical Complexity

Lexical complexity measures the quantitative and qualitative aspects of vocabulary usage in a text, often used to assess L2 (second language) writing proficiency (Bulté & Housen, 2012).

## Dimensions of Lexical Complexity

Lexical complexity is typically categorized into four dimensions (Zhong & Yang, 2025):

### 1. Lexical Density
Measures the informational content of a text based on the ratio of specific word classes.
- **R-Tok-CW (Ratio of Content Words)**: $\frac{\text{Tokens of (Nouns + Verbs + Adjectives + Adverbs)}}{\text{Total Tokens}}$
- **R-Tok-IW (Ratio of Independent Words)**: Includes content words plus pronouns, conjunctions, and exclamations.

### 2. Lexical Diversity
Measures the richness or variety of the vocabulary (quantity of different words).
- **Type-Token Ratio (TTR)**: $\frac{\text{Types}}{\text{Tokens}}$ (Sensitive to text length).
- **Uber Index**: A length-invariant measure of lexical diversity.
  $$\text{Uber} = \frac{(\log \text{Tokens})^2}{\log \text{Tokens} - \log \text{Types}}$$
- **Standardized Type-Token Ratio (STTR)**: The mean of TTR calculated for fixed-size blocks (e.g., every 100 or 300 words) to mitigate length effects.

### 3. Lexical Sophistication
Measures the "depth" or quality of vocabulary, specifically the use of advanced or low-frequency words.
- **Ratio of Low-Frequency Words (LFW)**: Calculated based on frequency lists (e.g., words not in the top 5,000 of a balanced corpus).
  $$\text{R-Typ-LFW} = \frac{\text{Types of Low-Frequency Words}}{\text{Total Types}}$$

### 4. Lexical Compositionality
Measures the formal structure and intricacy of the words themselves.
- **Average Form Length (AFL)**: Average number of characters per word lemma.
- **Average Pronunciation Length (APL)**: Average number of moras or kanas per word pronunciation.
