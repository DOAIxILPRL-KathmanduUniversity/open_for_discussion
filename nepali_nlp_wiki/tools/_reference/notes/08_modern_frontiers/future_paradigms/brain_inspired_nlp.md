# Brain-Inspired NLP

> [!IMPORTANT] Gist
> Traditional Deep Learning is often energy-intensive and opaque. Brain-inspired NLP looks to the neocortex for guidance, utilizing Sparse Distributed Representations (SDRs) to achieve noise-tolerant, energy-efficient intelligence (Soisoonthorn & Unger, 2026).

## 1. Biological Foundations
- **Energy Efficiency**: The human brain operates on ~20W of power while performing massive parallel processing across 86 billion neurons.
- **The Neocortex**: A uniform structure organized in a hierarchy, performing auto-associated memory and continuous prediction.
- **Hawkins' Thousand Brains Framework**: Intelligence is not a single model but thousands of models (columns in the neocortex) working in parallel to learn the structure of the world.

## 2. Sparse Distributed Representations (SDRs)
SDRs are the "language" of the brain, characterized by:
- **Sparsity**: Only a small percentage of neurons (or bits) are active at once (e.g., 2%).
- **Noise Tolerance**: Because information is distributed, the system can tolerate significant bit errors or "noise" without losing the original meaning.
- **Mathematical Capacity**: SDR vectors have an astronomical capacity to represent unique concepts with minimal false positives.
- **Overlap (Similarity)**: Concepts that are semantically similar have overlapping active bits in their SDR vectors, allowing for natural similarity measures.

## 3. Brain-Inspired Sequence Learning
- **Beyond Episodic Learning**: Unlike static datasets, biological systems learn through continuous interaction and sensory-motor integration.
- **Prediction as Core**: The brain is a "prediction machine" that constantly anticipates the next sensory input.
- **Applications**: SDR-based systems have shown high efficiency in Thai word segmentation and robust spelling correction (Soisoonthorn & Unger, 2026).
