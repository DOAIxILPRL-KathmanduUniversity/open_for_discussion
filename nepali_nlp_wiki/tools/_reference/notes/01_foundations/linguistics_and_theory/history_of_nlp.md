# History of NLP: A Timeline

> [!IMPORTANT] Gist
> NLP evolved from 1940s machine translation efforts to symbolic AI in the 60s, logical/grammatical frameworks in the 80s, statistical dominance in the 90s, and the current Neural/Transformer era.

## 1. The Early Years (1940s – 1950s)
- **Machine Translation (MT)**: Work began in the late 40s (Booth, 1967). Dominant languages were Russian and English.
- **Georgetown Experiment (1954)**: Fully automatic translation of sixty Russian sentences into English.

## 2. The Symbolic Era (1960s – 1970s)
- **BASEBALL (1961)**: The first question-answering system (Green et al.).
- **ELIZA (1966)**: A simulation of a Rogerian psychotherapist using pattern matching.
- **LUNAR (1978)**: A natural language interface for a moon rocks database (Woods).
- **SHRDLU (1970s)**: Winograd's system that operated in a "blocks world" with sophisticated linguistic and task processing.

## 3. The Grammatico-Logical Era (1980s)
- **Computational Grammar Theory**: Active research in logic for meaning and user beliefs/intentions.
- **Core Language Engine (SRI)**: General-purpose sentence processors.
- **Alvey Tools**: Development of practical resources, grammars, and parsers (Briscoe et al., 1987).

## 4. The Statistical Revolution (1990s – 2000s)
- **Empiricism**: Shift from rule-based to statistical models (Manning & Schütze, 1999).
- **Evaluation Trends**: DARPA conferences emphasized heavy evaluation in Information Extraction (MUC) and Speech Recognition.
- **Web Scaling**: Growth of unsupervised and semi-supervised learning algorithms to handle internet-scale data.

## 5. The Neural & Transformer Era (2010s – Present)
- **Deep Learning**: Rise of CNNs/RNNs for sequence modeling.
- **The Transformer (2017)**: Parallelizable attention mechanisms.
- **LLMs**: Massively scaled models (GPT, BERT) achieving human-level performance on many benchmarks.
