# Computational Social Science (CSS) with Text

> [!IMPORTANT] Gist
> Text is a proxy for human faculty. It encodes sociocultural and psychological constructs—trust, power, beliefs, and fears—allowing social scientists to measure society's attitudes over time at a scale previously impossible (Hovy, 2020).

## 1. Measuring Sociocultural Constructs
Text analysis enables the quantification of abstract social variables:
- **Power & Influence**: Analyzing word choice and turn-taking in parliamentary proceedings or corporate emails to identify hierarchies (Prabhakaran et al., 2012).
- **Trust & Deception**: Identifying linguistic markers of reliability in online reviews or written correspondence (Niculae et al., 2015).
- **Historical Attitudes**: Using diachronic corpora (e.g., Google Books) to track how the perception of concepts like "gender" or "work" has shifted over decades (Hamilton et al., 2016).

## 2. Demographic Inference
Language reflects the identity of the author (Labov, 1972):
- **Age & Gender**: Specific lexical and syntactic patterns correlate with demographic factors.
- **Geographic Origin**: Detecting dialectal variation and regional slang.
- **Psychological Profiles**: Function words (pronouns, prepositions) can reveal an author's emotional stability, social integration, and cognitive complexity (Pennebaker, 2011).

## 3. The "Unstructured" Misnomer
While text is often called "unstructured," it is governed by deep regularities:
- **Syntactic Rules**: Grammatical structures that constrain how information can be encoded.
- **Social Context**: The "Semantic Choice" (Halliday) where the choice of one word over a synonym reveals the author's social positioning.

## 4. Methodological Workflow for Social Scientists
1. **Discovery**: Using unsupervised methods (Clustering, Topic Models) to find latent structures in the data.
2. **Exploration**: Visualizing these structures (e.g., via **t-SNE**) to identify clusters of similar documents or terms.
3. **Hypothesis Testing**: Using supervised methods (Classification) to test if specific linguistic markers predict social outcomes.
