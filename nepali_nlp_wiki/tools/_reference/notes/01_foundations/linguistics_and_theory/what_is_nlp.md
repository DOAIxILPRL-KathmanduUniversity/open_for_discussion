# What is NLP?

## Key Ideas
- Intersection of linguistics, computer science, and AI
- Goal: enable machines to understand, interpret, and generate human language
- Sub-fields: computational linguistics, speech processing, text mining

## History and Evolution
- **1950s**: Turing Test, Georgetown experiment (rule-based).
- **1990s**: Statistical revolution; introduction of HMMs and N-grams (Shieber, 1994).
- **2010s**: Neural revolution; Word2Vec (2013), LSTMs, and Attention.
- **2017+**: The Transformer era; BERT, GPT, and LLMs.

## The Core Philosophy: Ambiguity Resolution
"Linguistics is the eye and computation is the body." The central task of NLP is **Ambiguity Resolution**—determining the intended meaning among multiple possibilities at every level of language (Bhattacharyya & Joshi, 2023).

## Three Generations of NLP
NLP has evolved through three distinct phases of technology:
1. **First Generation (Rule-Based)**: Focus on symbolic logic, manually crafted grammars, and belongingness to a set of rules (e.g., ALICE, ELIZA).
2. **Second Generation (Statistical/Probabilistic)**: Use of large corpora and probability theory to find the "most likely" interpretation (e.g., HMMs, SMT, IBM Models).
3. **Third Generation (Neural/Dense)**: Leverages deep learning, Transformers (BERT/GPT), and dense vector representations to capture nuanced context.

## The Heart of Generative AI
At its core, Generative AI (GAI) is governed by **Probability Distribution**. 
- **Pattern Learning**: Models "munch" through billions of data points to learn the underlying laws and patterns.
- **Probability Estimation**: GAI estimates the likelihood of occurrences to generate novel data points (text, images, sound) that mimic the training distribution.
- **Mimicry vs. Creativity**: By sampling from this distribution, machines produce content that appears visually and linguistically identical to human-generated work.

## Analytical Layers
NLP processes language through hierarchical layers, each with its own ambiguities:
- **Phonetic/Phonological**: Ambiguity in sound waves.
- **Morphological**: Ambiguity in word formation.
- **Lexical**: Ambiguity in word sense.
- **Syntactic**: Ambiguity in structure.
- **Semantic**: Ambiguity in literal meaning.
- **Pragmatic**: Ambiguity in intent/context.
- **Discourse**: Ambiguity in multi-sentence coherence.

## Ambiguity and Challenges
- **Lexical Ambiguity**: "Bank" (river vs. financial).
- **Syntactic Ambiguity**: "I saw the man with the telescope."
- **Pragmatic Ambiguity**: "Can you open the window?" (Question vs. Request).

## Why It Matters
- Search engines, translation, chatbots, accessibility
- Low-resource languages like Nepali need NLP the most

## Machine Intelligence & Creativity
Modern NLP is moving beyond simple "understanding" to **Machine Intelligence**:
- **Interactive Intelligence**: Machines empowered to be interactive and intelligent in operations, offerings, and outputs.
- **AI Creativity**: The ability of machines to generate "creative" content (e.g., Portrait of Bellamy, The Next Rembrandt) using Generative AI (GANs, VQGAN + CLIP).
- **Software-Defined Machines**: Vision-enabling and NLP-enabling modules embedded in hardware to make machines surround- and situation-aware.
