# Qualitative Text Analysis

> [!IMPORTANT] Gist
> Qualitative text analysis is a systematic approach to interpreting meaning within texts through the construction of categories. It bridges the gap between raw data and theoretical insights through methodical rigor (Kuckartz, 2014).

## 1. Philosophical Foundations
- **Classical Hermeneutics**: The art of interpretation. It emphasizes the "Hermeneutic Circle"—understanding the whole text through its parts and the parts through the whole.
- **Grounded Theory**: An inductive methodology where theory is "grounded" in the systematic analysis of data, rather than being formed a priori.

## 2. Category Construction
Categories are the central building blocks of qualitative analysis.
- **Deductive Categories**: Derived from existing theories or research questions (top-down).
- **Inductive Categories**: Emerging directly from the text during the analysis process (bottom-up).
- **Process**: Initial work $\to$ Memos $\to$ Category Construction $\to$ Coding $\to$ Summarizing.

## 3. Basic Methods
Kuckartz (2014) identifies three core methods:
1. **Thematic Qualitative Analysis**: Identifying and organizing common themes across multiple cases. Uses a **Thematic Matrix** to summarize coded segments.
2. **Evaluative Qualitative Analysis**: Assessing the intensity or characteristics of a specific phenomenon (e.g., "Sense of Responsibility") using a defined scale.
3. **Type-Building Analysis**: Reducing the diversity of cases to a set of types or a typology (e.g., categorizing environmental behavior into "Idealists," "Realists," etc.).

## 4. QDA Software (Computer-Assisted)
Qualitative Data Analysis (QDA) software (like MAXQDA, NVivo) assists in:
- **Transcription**: Using systems like the **Jefferson Notation** to capture not just words but also tone, pauses, and emphasis.
- **Coding & Retrieval**: Managing thousands of text segments and their assigned categories.
- **Visualization**: Creating **Profile Matrices**, Summary Grids, and group discussion maps to identify patterns.

## 5. Quality Standards
- **Inter-coder Reliability**: The degree to which different analysts agree on the coding of the same text.
- **Methodical Rigor**: Ensuring that the analysis process is transparent, documented, and reproducible.
