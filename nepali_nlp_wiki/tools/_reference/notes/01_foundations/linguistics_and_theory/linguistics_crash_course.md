# Linguistics Crash Course

## Key Concepts
- Phonology — sound systems
- Morphology — word structure (stems, affixes)
- Syntax — sentence structure (parse trees, grammar)
- Semantics — meaning
- Pragmatics — context-dependent meaning

## NLP-Relevant Grammar Concepts
- **Transitive Verbs & Direct Objects**: In intent extraction, the verb (action) and its direct object (target) are often the most critical components (e.g., "Order [verb] pizza [dobj]").
- **Modal Auxiliary Verbs**: Words like "can," "should," and "will" that indicate likelihood, permission, or advice. Essential for reconstructing sentences from questions.
- **Personal Pronouns**: Mapping pronouns (I, me, you, it) back to their referents (Nominal Subjects or Objects) is a key task in **Reference Resolution**.
- **Prepositional Objects**: Prepositions (in, for, to) link noun phrases to semantic roles (spatial, temporal).

## Why Linguists Matter for NLP
- Annotation guidelines come from linguistic theory.
- Morphologically rich languages (Nepali, Turkish) need deeper analysis.
- Syntax trees underpin parsing algorithms.
