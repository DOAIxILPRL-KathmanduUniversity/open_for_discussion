# Corpora & Linguistic Resources

> [!IMPORTANT] Gist
> A corpus is a large, structured collection of machine-readable texts used for linguistic analysis and training NLP models. The quality and diversity of the corpus define the boundaries of what a model can learn (Kurdi, 2016).

## 1. Corpus Taxonomy
Corpora can be classified based on several criteria:
- **Written vs. Spoken**: Spoken corpora require audio recording and transcription (manual or ASR).
- **Monolingual vs. Multilingual**: Parallel corpora (sentence-aligned translations) are essential for Machine Translation.
- **Diachronic vs. Synchronic**: Diachronic corpora track language change over time; synchronic corpora focus on a single point in time.
- **Thematic Representativity**: General-purpose (e.g., Brown Corpus) vs. Domain-specific (e.g., medical or legal).

## 2. The Lifecycle of a Corpus
1.  **Needs Analysis**: Defining the target domain and language.
2.  **Scenario Design**: Planning how to collect the data.
3.  **Collection**: Harvesting text via web scraping, OCR, or direct human recording.
4.  **Transcription/Cleaning**: Converting audio/scans into clean text.
5.  **Annotation**: Adding linguistic metadata (POS tags, NER, syntax trees).
6.  **Documentation**: Recording metadata about the collection process.
7.  **Statistical Analysis**: Checking for Zipf's Law distribution and vocabulary coverage.

## 3. Major Resource Agencies
- **LDC (Linguistic Data Consortium)**: Based at UPenn; provides massive sets of annotated data.
- **ELRA (European Language Resource Agency)**: Focuses on multilingual resources for the EU.
- **Gutenberg Project**: A major source for copyright-free classical literature.
