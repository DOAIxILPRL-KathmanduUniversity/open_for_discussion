# Textual Complexity & Cohesion

> [!IMPORTANT] Gist
> Textual complexity and cohesion are critical indicators of readability and comprehension. Analyzing these requires moving beyond surface-level statistics to deep semantic and discourse-level features (Dascalu, 2014).

## 1. Cohesion vs. Coherence
- **Cohesion**: The formal, linguistic links that hold a text together (e.g., anaphora, connectives, lexical overlap). It is an objective property of the text.
- **Coherence**: The mental representation of the text's meaning in the reader's mind. It depends on the reader's background knowledge and cognitive processing.
- **The Cohesion-Coherence Paradox**: Increasing local cohesion (e.g., adding more connectives) can sometimes *hinder* comprehension for high-knowledge readers while helping low-knowledge ones.

## 2. Textual Complexity Metrics
Dascalu (2014) identifies a multi-layered approach to complexity:
- **Surface Analysis**: Sentence length, word length, syllable counts.
- **Morphology & Syntax**: Part-of-speech distributions, dependency tree depth, syntactic complexity.
- **Semantics**: Word polysemy (WordNet), semantic similarity between sentences (LSA/LDA).
- **Discourse/Cohesion**: Lexical chains, argument overlap, and latent semantic cohesion.

## 3. The Polyphonic Model (Dialogism)
Inspired by Bakhtin, this model treats discourse as an intertwining of "voices":
- **Voice Inter-animation**: The degree to which different participants or ideas in a discourse interact, echo, and build upon each other.
- **Social Knowledge-Building**: Analyzing collaboration in CSCL (Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning) by measuring how participants' "voices" evolve and influence the group's collective understanding.
- **ReaderBench**: A system that uses these models to assess individual involvement and collaboration through cohesion and dialogical analysis.
