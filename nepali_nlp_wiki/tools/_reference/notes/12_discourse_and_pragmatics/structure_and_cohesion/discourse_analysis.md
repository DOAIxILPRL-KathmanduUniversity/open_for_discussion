# Discourse Analysis & Written Text Structure

> [!IMPORTANT] Gist
> Discourse analysis moves beyond the sentence to examine how texts are organized, how meaning is constructed through interaction, and how knowledge is valued within specific genres (Coulthard, 1994).

## 1. Text Grammar & Discourse Theory
A text is more than a sequence of sentences; it is a coherent unit governed by rules that operate beyond the sentence level (van Dijk & Petöfi, 1977).
- **Text Grammar**: A formal framework for describing the linguistic structure of coherent texts, including anaphoric/referential relationships and sentential adverbs.
- **Connectives**: Logico-semantic relations (e.g., *because*, *however*, *consequently*) that create the "Connective" structure of a text, linking clauses into larger macro-structures.
- **Semantic Choice**: The view that a text represents a set of choices made within a social context, where each choice reflects the speaker's goals and social positioning (Halliday, 1977).

## 2. Clause Relations & Information Structure
How sentences connect to form a cohesive message:
- **Problem-Solution Pattern**: A common discourse structure in technical and scientific writing.
- **Theme and Rheme**: 
    - **Theme**: The "starting point" of the message (usually given information).
    - **Rheme**: The remainder of the message (usually new information).
    - Efficient discourse often uses the Rheme of one sentence as the Theme of the next (Thematic Progression).

## 2. Nominal Group Lexical Cohesion
The use of nouns and nominalizations to "label" previous segments of discourse (e.g., using "This discovery..." to refer back to three sentences describing a result).

## 3. Genre Analysis (ESP)
English for Specific Purposes (ESP) focuses on the unique structures of different text types:
- **Scientific Discourse**: The "Grammar of Science" (Halliday) where nominalization and passive voice create a facade of objective truth.
- **Editorials**: The structure of persuasion and ideological framing in newspapers.

## 4. Interaction in Written Text
The view that written text is a "slow-motion" interaction between writer and reader.
- **Predictive Categories**: Signalling what will come next in the text (e.g., "There are three reasons..." predicts a list).
- **Contextual Monitoring**: How readers maintain a "Frame of Reference" across a narrative.

## 5. Multimodal Discourse Analysis (MDA)
Meaning is often created through the co-deployment of multiple semiotic resources (Baldry & Thibault, 2006).
- **Resource Integration Principle**: Text, images, and layout (actional semiotics) work together to create a unified meaning. 
- **Thematic Progression in MDA**: In web pages or film, the "Theme" might be an image, and the "Rheme" is the text or narration that explains it.
- **Cluster Analysis**: Identifying groups of multimodal objects (e.g., a header + an image + a button) that function together as a single discourse unit.

## 6. AI-Driven Discourse Variation
- **Paraphrasing Bias**: AI tools (like ChatGPT) can disrupt the original discourse structure by altering **Discourse Markers**. This can shift the logical connection between ideas (e.g., changing "However" to "And" changes the contrastive relation).
- **Corrective Feedback**: AI systems act as "automated tutors," providing feedback on coherence and cohesion by suggesting better discourse transitions.

## 7. Lexical Priming & Literary Creativity
Words are "primed" to occur in specific contexts, with specific meanings, and with specific neighbors (Michael Hoey).
- **Lexical Priming**: The theory that our knowledge of a word is actually a knowledge of the "patterns" it inhabits (collocation, semantic association, etc.). 
- **Creative Deviation**: Literary creativity often arises from "breaking" these primings (e.g., using a word with a typically negative semantic prosody in a positive context).

## 8. Corpus Stylistics
Bridging the gap between linguistics and literary criticism by using quantitative data to support qualitative interpretations.
- **Local Textual Functions**: Analyzing how specific words function differently within a particular text or "small world" (e.g., the function of the word "sustainable" in a specific corporate report).
- **Multi-word Sequences**: Identifying "clusters" or "lexical bundles" that characterize a specific author's style or a specific literary genre.

## 9. Electronic Text Techniques
Using computers to discover patterns in large collections (Adolphs, 2006):
- **Keywords**: Words that occur with significantly higher frequency in a specific text compared to a general corpus (revealing focus and ideology).
- **Collocation & Mutual Information (MI)**: Measuring the statistical likelihood of two words appearing together (e.g., "cause" often collocates with negative outcomes like "cancer" or "damage").
- **Semantic Prosody**: The "aura of meaning" a word acquires from its typical collocates (e.g., the word "commit" has a negative prosody because it usually precedes "crime," "suicide," or "error").
