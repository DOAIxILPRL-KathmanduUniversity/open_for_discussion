# Discourse & Pragmatic Analysis

> [!IMPORTANT] Gist
> Discourse analysis focuses on meaning across multiple sentences (coherence/cohesion), while Pragmatics focuses on meaning in context (intent, speech acts, and social norms).

## 1. Discourse Analysis: Cohesion and Structure
- **Cohesion**: The linguistic ties that hold a text together (e.g., conjunctions, repetition).
- **Coherence**: The underlying logical structure that makes a text meaningful.
- **Reference Resolution**: Determining which entity a word refers to (e.g., resolving "it" in "The animal didn't cross the road because it was too tired").
- **Discourse Structure**: Navigating the hierarchy of a document to establish logical flow.
- **Endophora**: Reference to something mentioned within the text (Anaphora vs. Cataphora).
- **Exophora**: Reference to something outside the text (contextual environment).
- **Schema & Script**: Cognitive frameworks for representing generic concepts and sequences of events.

## 2. Pragmatics: Meaning in Context
- **Speech Acts**: The actions performed via utterances.
    - **Locutionary**: The literal act of saying something.
    - **Illocutionary**: The intended communicative purpose (e.g., a request).
    - **Perlocutionary**: The actual effect on the listener (e.g., getting the salt).
    - **Indirect Speech Acts**: Using a question about ability ("Can you reach...") to perform a command.
- **Common Ground**: The shared knowledge and beliefs between dialogue participants that allow for abbreviated communication and resolution of anaphora.
- **Commitment**: In strategic conversation, interlocutors make "Public Commitments" which may differ from their private cognitive states.
- **Information Status**: Distinguishing between **Given** (old) and **New** information, often marked by intonation or syntax (e.g., using "the" vs. "a").
